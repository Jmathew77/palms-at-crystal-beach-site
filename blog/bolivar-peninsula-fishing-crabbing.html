Fishing & crabbing

Fishing and Crabbing on Bolivar Peninsula: A Visitor's Guide

By The Palms at Crystal Beach RV Park · November 14, 2026 · 6 min read

Surf, bay and pier fishing, plus crabbing, are a big part of life on Bolivar Peninsula. Here's where to start, what you can catch, and the license and crab rules to check first.

Fishing is a big reason guests choose Bolivar Peninsula. You can fish the surf, the bay shoreline and, if you stay at The Palms at Crystal Beach RV Park, our own fishing and crabbing pier. This guide covers where to start, what you can catch, license basics and the crabbing rules to check before you set a trap.

Where to fish near Crystal Beach

The Gulf surf

The peninsula's open beaches give surf anglers plenty of room. Galveston County allows driving on the beach, and a vehicle parked on the sand needs a Bolivar Beach Parking Permit.

The bay side

On the north side of the peninsula, East Galveston Bay offers shoreline and shell-bed fishing. A guide report from early November 2026 described anglers working shoreline shell beds for trout and redfish.

The Palms pier

You don't have to leave the park to fish or crab. We have a fishing and crabbing pier for guests. Ask the office about current pier guidelines when you arrive.

Rollover Pass

Rollover Pass has long been a famous bank-fishing spot. It has been closed for a restoration project, and the Texas General Land Office had estimated completion for July 2026. We haven't confirmed a reopening, so check with a local bait shop or the Bolivar Peninsula tourism site before planning a trip around it.

What you can catch

The Bolivar Peninsula tourism site lists these as common catches from the banks at the pass: croaker, flounder, redfish, sand trout, speckled trout, black drum, sheepshead, saltwater catfish and Spanish mackerel. Anglers often point to fall as a strong season for redfish and spring for spotted seatrout, though catches vary with weather, water temperature and tides. A local bait shop will give you the best current report.

Fishing guides and charters also operate on the peninsula if you'd like help finding fish.

Do you need a Texas fishing license?

Most anglers 17 and older need a Texas fishing license with the right saltwater coverage. According to 2026–27 fee listings, a non-resident saltwater package runs about $63 for the license year, and a non-resident one-day all-water license is about $16. Fees change, and there are exemptions, so confirm everything on the Texas Parks and Wildlife Department website before you buy.

Crabbing: rules to check first

Blue crabbing is a popular, low-cost way to spend an afternoon. Texas has specific rules, and sources disagree on the details, so read the official TPWD crab regulations first. The points to look for:

  • Personal use only. TPWD says crabs taken with a recreational license can't be sold.
  • Minimum size. Crabs generally must measure at least five inches across the widest part of the shell, so bring a gauge.
  • Trap rules. Limits on the number of traps, trap marking and temporary closure periods apply to anyone using traps.
  • License questions. Check TPWD for what your method requires.

Practical tips

  • Bring a cooler with ice for your catch and a bucket for crabs.
  • Check the wind and weather. Gulf conditions change quickly.
  • Wear sun protection and bring water, even in winter.
  • Stop at a local bait shop for tackle and a current fishing report.

Fishing as part of a longer stay

For long-term guests, low-cost recreation matters. Fishing and crabbing cost little beyond a license and bait, and monthly rent at The Palms is all bills paid. That's one reason retirees and long-term guests enjoy it here. Learn more in our guide to affordable long-term RV living in Texas, or read 10 reasons to choose Crystal Beach.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a Texas fishing license to fish near Crystal Beach?

Most anglers 17 and older need a Texas fishing license with saltwater coverage. Fees and exemptions change, so confirm on the Texas Parks and Wildlife Department website before you buy.

What can I catch from shore on Bolivar Peninsula?

Common catches from the banks include croaker, flounder, redfish, sand trout, speckled trout, black drum, sheepshead, saltwater catfish and Spanish mackerel. A local bait shop can tell you what's biting now.

Can I go crabbing at The Palms?

The Palms has a fishing and crabbing pier for guests. Ask the office about current pier guidelines, and follow the TPWD crab regulations on size, traps and licensing.

Is Rollover Pass open?

Rollover Pass has been closed for a restoration project, with the Texas General Land Office estimating completion for July 2026. Confirm its current status with a local bait shop or the Bolivar Peninsula tourism site before you go.
