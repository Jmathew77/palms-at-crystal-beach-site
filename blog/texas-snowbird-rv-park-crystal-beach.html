The Snowbird's Guide to Wintering in Crystal Beach, Texas

By The Palms at Crystal Beach RV Park · November 14, 2026 · 7 min read

Looking for a Texas snowbird RV park on the Gulf Coast without the crowds or the high prices? Here's what a winter in Crystal Beach on Bolivar Peninsula is really like: the weather, what to do, what it costs, and how to get ready.

Every winter, RVers from the Midwest, the Plains and Canada head south looking for three things: milder weather, a lower cost of living, and a friendly place to park for a few months. Most people think of Florida, Arizona or the Rio Grande Valley first. This guide is about a quieter option that often gets missed: Crystal Beach, on Texas's Bolivar Peninsula, a free ferry ride from Galveston.

We run The Palms at Crystal Beach RV Park (formerly Paula's Vineyard RV Park), so we're biased, but we've tried to give you an honest picture of what wintering here is like, including what to plan for.

Why snowbirds choose Crystal Beach for the winter

Mild Gulf Coast winters

Winter on the upper Texas coast is mild by northern standards. NOAA's 1991–2020 climate normals for nearby Galveston put the average January high at about 61°F and the average low around 45°F. Many winter days are sunny and in the 60s, which is fine for fishing, beach walks and sitting outside. It isn't the tropics, though. Cold fronts do blow through, and a few nights each winter can drop near freezing, so bring a jacket and a plan for your water hose (more on that below). Atlantic hurricane season runs June 1 through November 30, so a winter stay falls outside it; see our hurricane season guide.

A quiet beach town in the off-season

Crystal Beach fills up in summer, but from November through February the peninsula is calm. You get wide, uncrowded beaches, easy parking and local restaurants where staff learn your name. If you've wintered in a busy snowbird hub and felt packed in, Bolivar Peninsula is a real change of pace.

All-bills-paid monthly RV lot rent

Cost is a big reason people come back. At The Palms, monthly RV sites start at $450 a month for a standard pad and $500 a month for a premium concrete pad, and monthly rent is all bills paid: water, sewer, trash and free high-speed Wi-Fi are included, along with up to $125 of electricity every month. If you plan to stay all season, ask about our 6-month and 12-month discounts. See the full details on our monthly RV lot rentals page.

Winter birding at Bolivar Flats

Birders, this one is for you. The Bolivar Flats Shorebird Sanctuary, managed by Houston Audubon at the west end of the peninsula, is one of the most important shorebird sites on the Gulf Coast. Houston Audubon says hundreds of thousands of shorebirds use it each year to rest, feed and spend the winter. Expect big flocks of American white pelicans, plus plovers, sandpipers and wading birds. Protected species such as Piping Plovers and Red Knots spend time here as well. If you stay into spring, Houston Audubon's High Island sanctuaries just up the road are famous for spring songbird migration.

Fishing and crabbing

Fishing is a year-round draw on the peninsula, with surf and bay spots close by (our fishing and crabbing guide covers what's biting and the license basics). You can also drop a line or a crab trap from our own fishing and crabbing pier without leaving the park. Texas requires a fishing license for most anglers, and Texas Parks and Wildlife sells non-resident licenses for visitors.

Beach walks and beach driving

Bolivar Peninsula is one of the Texas beaches where you can drive on the sand. According to Galveston County, you can drive along the beach without a permit, but a vehicle parked on the beach needs a Bolivar Beach Parking Permit. These are sold at local businesses and the Bolivar Peninsula Chamber of Commerce.

Day trips to Galveston on the free ferry

The Galveston–Port Bolivar Ferry, run by TxDOT, is free, runs 24 hours a day, and takes about 18 minutes to cross. Dolphins often swim alongside. Towing an RV? Read our Bolivar ferry guide first. Winter is the best time to use it, because the long lines that build up on summer weekends and holidays usually aren't there. On the Galveston side you'll find the historic Strand district, museums, restaurants and the big Mardi Gras Galveston celebration in late winter.

History and local landmarks

Closer to home, visit Fort Travis Seashore Park in Port Bolivar and see the historic Bolivar Point Lighthouse (it's privately owned, so you view it from the road).

Snowbird checklist: wintering in an RV on the Texas coast

  • Book early. Monthly winter sites fill up. Call 936-666-3423 or request your dates online.
  • Plan for cold snaps. Bring a heated water hose or be ready to disconnect your hose on the few nights a hard freeze is forecast.
  • Heat smart. Monthly rent covers up to $125 of electricity. If you heat only with electric space heaters, you could go over on the coldest months. Using your propane furnace on cold nights and skirting or insulating your rig helps keep your usage under the allowance. If you do go over, only the overage is billed.
  • Bring a surge protector. Our park rules require a properly rated RV surge protector, which is smart insurance for your rig anyway.
  • Plan your mail. Mail and packages can't be received at the park without prior written management approval, so set up forwarding or ask the office before you arrive.
  • Read the park rules ahead of time. They're posted on our rules and regulations page.

Is Crystal Beach right for your winter?

If you want nightlife, golf courses and big-box stores at the end of every block, a larger snowbird town may suit you better. If you want mild weather, quiet beaches, great birding and fishing, and a winter that doesn't drain your budget, Crystal Beach is worth a serious look. Many guests who come for one winter end up booking the next before they leave.

Frequently asked questions

Is Crystal Beach, Texas a good place for snowbirds?

Yes. Crystal Beach has mild Gulf Coast winters (average January highs around 61°F), quiet off-season beaches, excellent winter birding at Bolivar Flats, good fishing, and a free ferry to Galveston. Monthly RV sites at The Palms start at $450, all bills paid: water, sewer, trash, Wi-Fi, and up to $125 a month of electricity.

How cold does it get in Crystal Beach in the winter?

Typical January days reach the low 60s with nights in the mid-40s. Cold fronts can bring a few nights near freezing each winter, so snowbirds should be ready to protect their water hose on those nights.

How much does a monthly winter RV site cost at The Palms?

Monthly RV sites are $450 for a standard pad and $500 for a premium concrete pad. Monthly rent is all bills paid (water, sewer, trash, free Wi-Fi, and up to $125 of electricity), and discounts are available for 6-month and 12-month stays.

Is electricity included for snowbirds staying monthly?

Yes. Monthly rates include up to $125 of electricity usage each month. If you use more, only the overage is billed. Using your propane furnace on cold nights helps keep usage under the allowance.

When should I book a winter RV site in Crystal Beach?

As early as you can. Monthly winter sites fill up, so call 936-666-3423 or request your dates online to check availability.
