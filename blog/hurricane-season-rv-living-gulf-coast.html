Hurricane Season and Long-Term RV Living on the Texas Gulf Coast

By The Palms at Crystal Beach RV Park · November 14, 2026 · 6 min read

Hurricane season is part of life on the Gulf Coast. Here is how RVers plan for it on Bolivar Peninsula: the calendar, evacuation, the ferry, a go-bag, and what our park rules say.

If you live or stay on the Texas Gulf Coast, hurricanes are part of the picture, and an honest RV park should say so. The good news is that an RV can move, hurricane season has a clear calendar, and a little planning goes a long way. This guide covers how RVers prepare for hurricane season on Bolivar Peninsula, and what the rules at The Palms at Crystal Beach RV Park say.

When is hurricane season?

The Atlantic hurricane season runs from June 1 through November 30. According to National Hurricane Center figures cited in news coverage, most activity falls between mid-August and mid-October, with a climatological peak around September 10. A winter stay, from December through spring, falls outside the season. See our snowbird guide to wintering in Crystal Beach.

Why locals take evacuation orders seriously

Hurricane Ike in September 2008 caused catastrophic damage across Bolivar Peninsula, including Crystal Beach, with reports of storm surge of 15 to 20 feet or more. The community has since rebuilt, and building practices have changed. We mention it not to frighten anyone but because it explains why people here act early when a storm threatens.

What happens when a storm threatens

  • Evacuation orders can come early. In 2020, ahead of Hurricane Laura, the Galveston County Judge ordered a mandatory evacuation of Bolivar Peninsula.
  • The ferry may stop. Ferry service to Galveston was suspended before Laura, and news reports said it shuts down when winds reach 45 mph or tides reach 4.5 feet. Don't build an evacuation plan around the ferry.
  • Roads can flood. During Laura, storm surge washed over Highway 87 where the peninsula meets the mainland and covered the road in rocks. That is a good reason to leave early.

Your evacuation plan

RV sources and Texas emergency guidance agree on the basics:

  • Know your route before you need it. Plan an inland route that suits the size of your rig, and avoid roads headed toward the storm's expected path. Texas's designated evacuation routes have fuel stations and emergency personnel along them. From Crystal Beach the inland route is Highway 87 to Highway 124 and north toward I-10 near Winnie, but confirm current guidance for any storm.
  • Leave when ordered, or sooner. If you're towing, leave early. Waiting until the roads are crowded makes the trip slower and riskier.
  • Fuel up early and keep your tow vehicle in good working order during the season.
  • Pack a go-bag with ID, insurance papers, medications, chargers, pet supplies and some cash, and keep it where you can grab it quickly.
  • Have backup lodging. Campgrounds and hotels fill up during an evacuation, so know where else you could go.
  • Know your insurance. Ask your insurer what your RV policy covers for wind, flood and towing, and keep photos of your rig.
  • Be ready to leave the RV. If you can get out faster with only a car or truck, your safety comes first.

Keep your rig road-ready in season

Between June and November, treat your RV as something you may need to move on short notice. Keep your tires in good condition, keep your hitch and tow vehicle ready, and avoid add-ons around your site that would be slow to take down. Tents, tarps and unapproved structures aren't allowed at The Palms anyway.

What our park rules say

  • You are responsible for monitoring the weather and protecting your RV, vehicles, property, family, visitors and pets.
  • Management may require guests to secure items, disconnect utilities, move vehicles, evacuate or remove RVs when necessary for safety or emergency response.
  • No refunds for weather. Prepaid amounts are non-refundable unless management agrees in writing, and no refunds or credits are given for weather, storms, flooding or ferry delays. Ask us about the policy before you book during the season.
  • Keep your contact details current. Your phone number, email address and mailing address must be on file so we can reach you.

Is Crystal Beach still worth it?

For many people, yes. Winters are mild and fall outside the season, the beaches are wide and quiet, and costs are low. The key is to plan: choose your travel dates with the calendar in mind, keep your rig road-ready from June to November, and follow evacuation orders promptly. Frequently asked questions

When is hurricane season on the Texas Gulf Coast?

The Atlantic hurricane season runs June 1 through November 30. Most activity falls between mid-August and mid-October.

Is it safe to winter in Crystal Beach?

A winter stay from December through spring falls outside hurricane season, though cold fronts and storms can still bring wind, rain and a few near-freezing nights.

What happens if a hurricane threatens while I'm staying at The Palms?

Our rules make guests responsible for monitoring the weather and protecting their property. Management may require guests to secure items, disconnect utilities, move vehicles, evacuate or remove RVs for safety. Follow county evacuation orders.

Will I get a refund if I have to leave because of a storm?

Under our rules, prepaid amounts are non-refundable unless management agrees in writing, and no refunds or credits are given for weather or storms. Call 936-666-3423 to ask about your situation before you book.

Does the Bolivar ferry run during an evacuation?

Not necessarily. Ferry service was suspended before Hurricane Laura in 2020, and news reports say it stops in high winds or high tides. Plan an inland route.
