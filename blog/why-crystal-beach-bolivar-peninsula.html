10 Reasons to Choose Crystal Beach and Bolivar Peninsula

By The Palms at Crystal Beach RV Park · November 14, 2026 · 6 min read

Looking for a quieter Texas beach town with a free ferry, great fishing and birding, and low living costs? Here are ten reasons people visit, winter and settle in Crystal Beach on Bolivar Peninsula.

Crystal Beach is a laid-back Gulf Coast beach town on Texas's Bolivar Peninsula, a free ferry ride from Galveston. People come for a weekend, winter for a season, or settle in for the long haul. We run The Palms at Crystal Beach RV Park (formerly Paula's Vineyard RV Park), and these are the ten reasons we hear most from guests.

1. Open beaches you can drive on

Bolivar Peninsula is one of the places in Texas where you can drive on the beach. According to Galveston County, you can drive along the sand without a permit, but a vehicle that is parked on the beach needs a Bolivar Beach Parking Permit. Permits are sold at local businesses and the Bolivar Peninsula Chamber of Commerce. Check the county page for current prices and rules before you park.

2. A free ferry to Galveston

The TxDOT-run Galveston–Port Bolivar Ferry costs nothing, runs around the clock, and crosses in about 18 minutes. It turns a day trip to Galveston's museums, restaurants and historic Strand district into an easy outing. Towing an RV? See our Bolivar ferry guide for RVers.

3. Fishing and crabbing close to your site

Surf, bay and bank fishing are all within reach, and The Palms has its own fishing and crabbing pier. Our fishing and crabbing guide covers what you can catch, license basics and the crabbing rules to check.

4. Excellent birding

The peninsula is part of the Great Texas Coastal Birding Trail. The Bolivar Flats Shorebird Sanctuary at the west end is a major site, and Houston Audubon says hundreds of thousands of shorebirds use it each year. Texas Parks and Wildlife's Bolivar Loop lists good stops along the way.

5. Mild winters

NOAA's 1991–2020 normals for nearby Galveston show an average January high near 61°F and a low around 45°F. Cold fronts still pass through, so it isn't tropical, but it is far gentler than a northern winter. See our snowbird guide to Crystal Beach for more.

6. History close by

Fort Travis Seashore Park in Port Bolivar is built around an 1898 coastal-artillery fort, with old concrete bunkers and gun emplacements to explore. Nearby, the Point Bolivar Lighthouse was built in 1872 and decommissioned in 1933. It is privately owned, so most visitors enjoy it from the road.

7. A small-town feel without big crowds

Summer weekends and holidays get busy, but the rest of the year Crystal Beach is quiet, with wide beaches and easy parking. The calendar includes events such as Jeep Weekend and a spring crab festival, and the Fourth of July brings fireworks along the beachfront. Dates change from year to year, so check with the Bolivar Peninsula Tourism and Visitors Center or CrystalBeach.com before you plan around one. For restaurants, live music and day trips, see our guide to things to do and where to eat.

8. Reachable from Houston

Most visitors from the Houston area allow roughly an hour and a half to two hours, depending on the route and whether they cross on the ferry. You can come by way of Galveston and the ferry, or inland by way of I-10 near Winnie and Highway 124. That makes Crystal Beach practical for a long weekend as well as a long stay.

9. Affordable stays

At The Palms, daily RV sites start at $50 a night and weekly stays are $200. Monthly lots are $450 for a standard pad and $500 for a premium concrete pad, and monthly rent is all bills paid: water, sewer, trash, free Wi-Fi and up to $125 of electricity a month. See the numbers in our RV lot vs. apartment cost comparison or on the monthly rentals page.

10. A park with the essentials

The Palms has 37 RV sites (29 premium concrete pads and 8 standard pads), gated access, free high-speed Wi-Fi, onsite laundry, outdoor showers, a community area and the fishing and crabbing pier. If you need more room for family or friends, there's also a 3 bed, 2 bath beach house rental on Airbnb.

What to know before you come

  • Hurricane season runs June 1 through November 30 on the Gulf Coast. Keep an eye on forecasts and have an evacuation plan if you visit then (see our hurricane season guide).
  • Summer weekends are busy. Expect crowds on the beach and longer waits at the ferry on weekends and holidays.
  • It's a quiet beach town, not a theme-park destination. If you want a slower pace, that's the point.

Frequently asked questions

Is Crystal Beach, Texas worth visiting?

Yes, if you want a quieter Gulf Coast beach town. Crystal Beach has wide beaches you can drive on, fishing and crabbing, excellent birding, a free ferry to Galveston, and mild winters. Summer weekends and holidays are the busiest times.

Can you drive on the beach in Crystal Beach, Texas?

Galveston County says you can drive along the beach without a permit, but a vehicle parked on the beach needs a Bolivar Beach Parking Permit. Check the county website for current rules and prices.

How far is Crystal Beach from Houston?

Most visitors allow roughly an hour and a half to two hours, depending on the route and any wait at the Galveston–Port Bolivar ferry. You can also drive inland by way of I-10 near Winnie and Highway 124.

What is the best time to visit Crystal Beach?

Spring and fall are comfortable, and winter is quiet and mild, with January highs near 61°F. Summers are hot, humid and busier, and hurricane season runs June 1 through November 30.

Where can I stay in Crystal Beach with an RV?
