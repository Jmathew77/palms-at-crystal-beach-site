Taking the Bolivar Ferry with an RV: What to Know Before You Go

By The Palms at Crystal Beach RV Park · November 14, 2026 · 5 min read

The free Galveston–Port Bolivar Ferry can save you a long drive around the bay. Here are the size limits, the wait times to expect, and the inland route if you'd rather skip the line.

If you're towing or driving an RV to Crystal Beach from Houston or Galveston, the free ferry between Galveston Island and Port Bolivar can save you a long drive around the bay. Here is what to know before you line up. It covers size limits, waits, timing and the inland alternative.

Ferry quick facts

  • Cost: free, run by TxDOT
  • Hours: 24 hours a day, though overnight capacity is lower than in the daytime
  • Crossing time: about 18 minutes on the water, plus loading and unloading
  • Posted vehicle limits: 65 ft long, 13.5 ft high, 8.5 ft wide, 80,000 lb
  • Busy-season waits: can be two hours or more
  • Ferry office: (409) 795-2230

Will your RV fit?

TxDOT's Galveston–Port Bolivar Ferry page lists these limits: a maximum length of 65 feet, a maximum height of 13.5 feet, a maximum width of 8.5 feet and a maximum weight of 80,000 pounds. Measure your whole rig, tow vehicle included, and know your height with the A/C unit and any roof gear.

One caution: TxDOT's separate ferry rules page lists different dimensions. If your rig is large or close to any limit, call the ferry office at (409) 795-2230 and confirm before you go. Review the ferry rules too.

How long will it really take?

The crossing itself is about 18 minutes. TxDOT figures cited by the Texas Legislative Budget Board compare that to about 2 hours and 20 minutes to drive around Galveston Bay. The real variable is the wait. TxDOT says waits can reach two hours or more in the high-traffic season, and peak periods include summer weekends and holidays.

When to cross

  • Avoid summer weekends and holidays if you can. Weekday mornings and off-season travel are usually far easier, and event weekends such as Jeep Weekend and July 4th are the busiest of all.
  • Check conditions before you leave. TxDOT's ferry page has schedule information and alerts for high-traffic periods.
  • Be careful overnight. TxDOT notes that fewer vessels run overnight, so expect lower capacity.

What to expect at the dock

Ferry attendants direct vehicles into lanes. Accounts from RVers describe larger rigs being sent to a particular lane, and being asked to wait for the next boat when a vessel is nearly full. Follow the attendants' directions, stay in your lane, and allow extra time so a missed boat doesn't wreck your plans. Retract slides and secure awnings, antennas and loose items as you would for any road trip.

The inland alternative: I-10, Winnie and Highway 124

You don't have to take the ferry. From I-10 near Winnie, Highway 124 runs south toward the peninsula and joins Highway 87. Travelers coming from Beaumont or Louisiana usually find this the natural route, and it can be worth considering from Houston when the ferry line is long. It adds miles, but not a wait for a boat.

Arriving at The Palms

Put 1250 N Crystal Beach Road, Crystal Beach, TX 77650 into your GPS. Call 936-666-3423 before you head out for arrival instructions, especially if you're in a long rig. Tell us your RV's length when you book so we can place you on the right pad.

Using the ferry as a guest

Once you're settled, the ferry becomes one of the best things about staying here. Because it's free, a day trip to Galveston costs only your gas and lunch. Dolphins often swim alongside the boat. See our snowbird guide and 10 reasons to choose Crystal Beach for ideas on the other side.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Galveston&ndash;Port Bolivar ferry free?

Yes. The ferry is operated by TxDOT and is toll-free.

Can RVs and trailers take the Bolivar ferry?

Yes, within the posted limits. TxDOT's ferry page lists a maximum length of 65 feet, height of 13.5 feet, width of 8.5 feet and weight of 80,000 pounds. Call the ferry office at (409) 795-2230 to confirm if your rig is large or close to a limit.

How long is the wait for the Bolivar ferry?

It varies. The crossing takes about 18 minutes, but TxDOT says waits can be two hours or more in the high-traffic season, including summer weekends and holidays.

Is the Bolivar ferry open 24 hours?

Yes, TxDOT operates it 24 hours a day, though fewer vessels run overnight.

How do I reach Crystal Beach without the ferry?

Drive inland by way of I-10 near Winnie, then south on Highway 124 to Highway 87. It is longer in miles but has no wait for a boat.
